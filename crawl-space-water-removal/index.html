Crawl Space Water Removal Help in Richland

A flooded crawl space rarely announces itself. Most people find it when the floors feel cold and damp, a musty smell comes up through the vents, or someone opens the access hatch and sees a pond. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. Our team pumps the water out, clears what is ruined and dries the framing before it softens.

Getting the water out is only the first half. Because lumber under a floor is slow to soak and slow to dry, our crew tracks its moisture content with a meter and runs dehumidification for the 3 to 5 days it normally takes to bring the readings down. Our team traces the source too, whether that is a supply line leak, a drain, or rain driven groundwater, so the space stays dry afterward.

What It Looks Like

  • An odor like damp soil that gets stronger close to the registers
  • Sweating pipes and ductwork that drip onto the ground beneath the home
  • Puddles or wet muck visible just inside the crawl space door
  • Stains on the support piers and stem walls that show how high water stood
  • Hardwood boards upstairs starting to cup, with edges higher than the centers
  • White or dark fuzzy growth on joists, beams or the subfloor

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

What You Breathe Upstairs

Much of what you breathe on the main level drifts up from beneath the house, carrying any mold spores and musty smells along with it.

Shifting Piers and Supports

Long periods of standing water loosen the soil that holds up the support piers. Sloping floors or sticking doors in the rooms above may point to this.

Contaminated Water

Do not assume the water below your floor is clean. If it came from a drain pipe or from waste laden groundwater it is rated Category 2 or 3, so we suit up and disinfect.

Weakened Floor Framing

The first soft wood is usually hidden, up where joists meet the subfloor or back at the sill plate. Checking with a probe is how we catch sections that have gone soft.

How the Damage Progresses

First day

Puddles Form Underneath

The dirt floor starts absorbing moisture as puddles form in every dip. Humidity in the space jumps within hours as the insulation pulls water into itself.

24 to 48 hours

Early Mold Growth

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

The moisture starts reaching the part of the house you live in. The lumber goes on taking in moisture, a stale smell spreads and flooring can begin to cup.

Past one week

Wood Starts Rotting

Wood that stays wet begins to decay. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Inspect and Make Safe

Nobody enters until the space has been screened for shock risks, hanging ducts and contamination. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Remove Standing Water

We run pumps and extraction lines until the pooled water is gone, low pockets and trenches along the footings included. Mud and silt left on the liner are cleared at the same time.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

We bag up the wet insulation, damaged vapor barrier and loose debris and take it all outside. Exposed joists and subfloor are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial where growth has started or is likely.

4

Final Drying and Checks

Low profile air movers and a dehumidifier run under the house until the wood reaches a normal moisture reading. You get the closing moisture readings plus a plan for keeping water out, after we verify everything by meter.
